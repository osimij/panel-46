# How this designer tags

Learned from the 107 saves they tagged by hand (238 tags) in November 2026. Panel gives
you this guide with every read, along with the section's live tag list and recent
examples of their own tagging, so new tags they add are picked up too. Edit this file
to change how saves are tagged.

## What a tag is for

Tags are shelves, not descriptions. The profile already describes the save in detail;
a tag answers a different question: **which shelf would I go to when I want this
again?** The designer comes back with a task in mind ("I need a chart", "show me
shader stuff", "interiors for the flat"), so every tag names a kind of work, a medium,
a craft, or a tool, and never a passing detail.

So tag the **reason it was saved**, not everything visible in it. A brand reel that
contains a phone screen isn't `mobile`. A landing page that has a chart isn't
`charts` unless the chart is the point.

## How many

Usually **1 or 2**, sometimes 3. Their counts: 32 saves with one tag, 42 with two, 20
with three, 13 with four or more. Go past three only when the designer's note names
several separate reasons (e.g. "unique shape and layout, the circle holds all the
navigation" gave six tags). One precise tag is better than three loose ones.

Work it out this way: pick the **one shelf** this belongs on. Then add a second or
third only if it's a separate reason they'd come looking for it, and you'd bet they
would tag it themselves. When unsure about a tag, leave it out: a missing tag costs
them less than a wrong one, because wrong tags pollute the shelves they browse.

## Where the reasons come from, strongest first

1. **Their note.** If there's a note, it's the tag list in prose. Map every thing it
   praises to a tag: "the colors" → `colors`; "the animation / transitions" →
   `animation`; "interface distortion, wave-glow" → `shader`; "chart design" →
   `charts`; "refreshing" → `refreshing`; "for our flat" → `interior design`. Don't
   add tags for things the note doesn't care about unless they're the obvious shelf
   (a dashboard note about colors still gets `charts` if it's a chart).
2. **What it is** (the shelf it would never be missing from): an office photo is
   `interior design`; an Illustrator how-to is `adobe-i`; a website is `web`.
3. **What the post text says it is**, when the pictures agree.

## Rules they follow

- **Lowercase, short, from the list.** Use the exact spelling of an existing tag
  (`3d`, `adobe-i`, `ps`, `ai-gen`, `launch-video`, `hero-section`, `motion design`).
  Never a synonym of a tag that exists (`color` → `colors`, `ui design` in the
  library → `ui`, `motion graphics` → `motion design`, `fonts` → `font`).
- **Medium first.** If a screen is the subject, it gets its platform: `web` for
  anything on a desktop browser or a desktop/macOS app, `mobile` for a phone app.
- **Moving work gets `animation`** when the movement is part of why it's good (UI
  motion, a logo transition, a morph, animated charts, scroll reveals, type in
  motion). It's their most used tag. Live-action film is not `animation`.
- **Tutorials get only their tool tag.** A how-to clip in Illustrator is just
  `adobe-i`; in Photoshop just `ps`. The exception: if the note keeps it for the
  *look* of the result ("I like the illustration style"), tag the look
  (`illustration`), not the tool.
- **Mood tags are rare.** The only feeling tag is `refreshing`, used when something
  feels new and unexpected, or when the note says so. Don't invent other moods
  (calm, premium, playful): that's what the profile's mood field is for.
- **No brand, person or platform names** as tags (no `apple`, `instagram`, `ios`).
- **No new tags, almost always.** The list below covers what they collect. Add one
  only when nothing on the list fits the main reason it was saved, and then name it
  the way they would: one or two plain lowercase words, the kind of work, never a
  mood or a brand.

## Where you'd likely get it wrong

These are the differences between what's visible and how they actually tag:

- **Don't add the interface family as filler.** `ui`, `ux`, `interface` and
  `interaction` are not given to every screen. A mobile animation is `mobile,
  animation`, not `mobile, interaction, animation, ui`. Use them only when the note
  praises the interface/usability/behavior, or the piece is clearly a UI-craft lesson.
- **A brand film is tagged by its motion, not as branding.** A case-study reel or
  brand reveal where the movement is the craft is `motion design` (often with
  `animation`), not `branding, identity`. Use `branding` / `identity` when the
  identity system itself is what's shown: logos, guideline pages, packaging, boards.
- **`poster` is for poster design.** A color study laid out as slides is just
  `colors`. A painting palette breakdown is just `colors`.
- **A product film:** a physical product presented on film (a phone launch film) is
  `presentation`; a software/AI product's launch or announcement film is
  `launch-video`. Add `cinematic` / `videography` only for live-action storytelling
  where the filming itself is the reason.
- **Effects reels aren't `web`.** A reel of WebGL experiments is `shader, animation`.
  `web` is for actual websites, web apps and browser UI.
- **Generative line textures are `illustration`**, with `animation` if they move, not
  `shader`, unless it's clearly a GPU/WebGL effect like a glow wave or distortion.
- **For brand pieces, tag the craft that makes it good.** An anniversary identity
  that's all about blackletter in motion is `animation, typography`, not `branding,
  identity, logo`. Reach for `branding` only when the brand system is the lesson.
- **A screen recording isn't automatically `animation`.** A scroll-through of a
  landing page or a walk through a dashboard is `web` (plus `charts`, etc.). Add
  `animation` when the movement is a highlight: a transition, a reveal, a morph.
- **The note can narrow it.** Bedding photos kept for "great colors combination" are
  just `colors`, not `interior design`. When the note says the one reason, that one
  reason is often the whole list. Without a note, tag every shelf it clearly belongs
  on: an olive-green mood board of interiors, cars and seats is `interior design,
  industrial design, object design, colors`.
- **Don't add `typography` or `colors` just because type or color is present.** Every
  design has both. Add them when they are why it was saved.

## The library (posts, images, videos)

**Interface**
- `web`: websites, landing pages, web apps, dashboards, desktop/macOS app UI, web
  interaction demos, WebGL sites, browser games.
- `mobile`: phone app screens and concepts, iOS interactions, phone UI explainers.
- `interaction`: the behavior is the lesson: a popover that reshapes, a command
  palette, a minimise effect, a flow that switches state. Usually with `web` or
  `mobile` and `animation`.
- `interface`: a screen or component kept as an interface reference, e.g. a widget, a
  modal, a dashboard card, a chat card. Often paired with `ui`.
- `ui`: the visual craft of the interface: layout, type, color, components.
- `ux`: how it works for the person: spacing that reads right, clear flows,
  navigation that's easy to grasp. They put `ux` with `ui` (`mobile, ux, ui`).
- `charts`: data visualization inside a product: charts, gauges, graphs, KPI cards.
- `onboarding`, `hero-section`, `footer`: that specific screen or section is the
  reference.

**Motion**
- `animation`: see the rule above. 33 saves.
- `motion design`: an authored motion-graphics piece: a brand film made of motion
  graphics, an explainer animation, an icon morph, a card-continuity transition. They
  usually pair it with `animation`.
- `shader`: WebGL/GLSL work: glow waves, distortion, refraction, liquid or noise
  transitions, 3D carousels on the GPU.
- `3d`: 3D is the subject: 3D asset libraries, 3D card flips, rendered objects.
- `launch-video`: a product launch or announcement film.
- `videography`, `cinematic`: live-action film craft (grading, lighting, camera).
- `ident`: a short motion ident or animated mark.

**Graphic**
- `colors`: the palette is a reason to keep it: their note mentions color, it's a
  color study or palette post, or one striking color combination carries the piece.
  Their second most used tag (22), so don't be stingy when color is clearly the draw.
- `illustration`: drawn or made imagery: line art, construction drawings, generative
  textures, 3D illustration, geometric poster art, an illustration style, archive
  imagery for image references.
- `typography`: type is the point: specimens, type-led layouts, type in motion,
  clean typographic UI.
- `font`: a typeface or font resource (a foundry, a font in use you'd go find).
- `branding`: brand identity work. `identity`: the identity system itself (marks,
  rules, patterns), usually together with `branding`.
- `presentation`: how work is presented: guideline decks, case-study boards, bento
  reels, product presentation films.
- `poster`: posters and poster-like editorial pieces (book spreads, carousels built
  as posters).
- `visual`: an art-direction or visual concept (e.g. a product revealed only by its
  shadow, a rejected hero concept, an image-led carousel).
- `logo`, `icons`, `packaging`: that artifact is the subject.

**Industrial**
- `interior design`: rooms, cafés, homes, interior details, materials in a space.
- `office design`: workspaces specifically (with `interior design`).
- `object design`: a designed object: lamps, sinks, holders, watches.
- `industrial design`: product/industrial design: manufactured objects, furniture,
  watches; often with `object design`.

**Tools** (for technique saves)
- `adobe-i`: Adobe Illustrator technique or tutorial.
- `ps`: Photoshop technique or tutorial.
- `ai-gen`: AI-generated images or film, AI filmmaking techniques.

**Other**
- `refreshing`: see the mood rule.
- `photography`, `clothing`, `portfolio`: exist for photography-led saves, fashion,
  and portfolio work.

## Sites

Sites are tagged with their own, smaller list. Almost every site gets `web`, plus the
reason it was kept: `animation` (their note praises transitions or scroll animation),
`scroll` and `motion` for scroll-driven storytelling, `typography` and `font` for
foundries and type-led sites, `illustration` for illustration-led or archive-image
sites, `shader` for WebGL, `colors`, `portfolio` for personal portfolios, `agency` and
`studio` for studio sites, `minimal` and `editorial` for the style when that's the
whole point. A type foundry is `font, typography`, not `web`.

## People & Accounts

People are tagged by **their discipline**, the reason you'd hire or follow them: `web
design`, `product design`, `brand identity`, `ui design`, `graphic design`. Give all
the ones their work shows, and nothing else.
