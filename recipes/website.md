# Website

For: a website or a landing page, new or a redesign.

## What makes it great

A great website is not a nice layout. It is these, working as one:

- **Words that say something.** In five seconds a visitor knows what this is, who it is for and why it is better. Nothing a competitor could paste onto their own site.
- **A structure with a reason.** Every section has one job, and the order answers the visitor's questions in the order they come up. No section is there because sites usually have one.
- **Color with a plan.** One accent, and a known place where it lives. Everything else stays out of its way.
- **Type judged in the real words**, in the project's language. Cyrillic and Tajik are set and looked at, never trusted.
- **Motion that explains or delights.** How the page moves is part of how it feels. Motion that only decorates is left out.
- **One gem.** One thing nobody else has, that people remember and show to someone: an illustration style, a shader, a physical object made real, a way of treating photos, one small interaction. One, not five. It belongs to this brand and could not sit on another site. Color, layout and motion grow from it, so the site feels like one piece.

If the page could be another company's by changing the logo, it is not done.

## Before the first step

Read the project card, the canvas (get_project) and get_verdicts. Then ask only what they don't already answer, at most four questions, in one message:

1. What is it, in one line, and who comes to it?
2. What is the one thing a visitor should do or understand before they leave?
3. What exists already: an old site, words, photos, a logo, a brand book? Ask them to add the files to the project (materials, brand assets) so you work from the real things.
4. How should it feel? Don't ask this in words alone. Find five or six saves in their library that feel different from each other, give them with the question (its saves) so they show in a row above the choices, and ask which are closest and which are wrong.

For a redesign, look at the old site first (the material or the link) and say in three lines: what works, what doesn't, what must stay.

Write what you learned as a note on the canvas (add_note), in their words. Then start_steps.

## Steps

### Sections

What the page shows, section by section, before any design.

- Decide the sections from the visitor's questions, in the order they come: what is this, is it for me, how does it work, can I trust it, what do I do now. Each section has one job. If you can't say a section's job in one line, it goes.
- Most pages need five to eight sections. Say why each one is there.
- Make a wireframe: make_sketch with kind "wireframe", titled "Wireframe", from the topic Sections. Start from the page below and fill it in: one row per section, with the note on the left (its name, its job, what is in it: which links, which buttons, what kind of picture or video) and gray blocks on the right.
- Gray only. No colors, no chosen fonts, no real copy yet: the placeholders say what the words will do ("Headline: the one promise", "Button: book a table").
- Name each section by what it is ("Hero", "Menu"). Don't number the sections, here or in any later sketch.
- This is not design. Don't polish it. It should take one try.

### Words

The real words for every section. Design comes after, around them.

- Read the designer's writing skill first (get_skill: the one that leads "writing"). Where it and these lines differ, the skill wins.
- Write in the project's language, in the voice of the people who will read it.
- The headline says what this is and why it matters to the reader, in words a person would say out loud. Test it: could a competitor use the same line? Then it says nothing. Write it again.
- Offer three headlines that come from different sides (what you get; what it ends; a plain claim with a fact or a number), not three wordings of one.
- Then, for each section: its heading, its text, its button labels. Short. A button says what happens when it is pressed.
- No filler. No "welcome to", "we are passionate", "solutions", "seamless", "next level", "unlock". No three adjectives in a row. No sentence that could be deleted without losing anything.
- Real names, numbers and facts from the project's materials. Never an invented number, client or quote. Where a fact is missing, say which, and ask.
- Put the words into the wireframe: a new sketch from it, "Wireframe with words", still gray (kind "wireframe"). The designer changes words in place in the studio. Build on their changes; don't put yours back.

Show the three headlines in your reply, then the sketch. Ask which headline. Then ask: go on to the gem, or change the words?

Decision to write down: the headline chosen, and the voice in a line ("plain, short, speaks to the accountant, no jokes").

### Gem

The one thing people will remember. It is decided before color and type, because they grow from it.

- Look for it in what is true about this product, its place or its people: an object they use, a gesture, a material, a document, a pattern from their culture, the thing the product does shown as a thing.
- Kinds of gem: an illustration style; a shader or a generated pattern; a real object in 3D or photographed; a way of treating photos; a typographic device; one interaction (a stamp that lands, a page that folds).
- Read the designer's rules again. Something physical made fully real is one way to a gem, and half-real is the failure.
- Search the library for how others did each kind (search_library with look), and read get_verdicts for what this designer keeps choosing and keeps turning down.
- Offer three ideas, different in kind. For each: one line saying what it is; one line saying why it belongs to this brand and no other; where it shows on the page (in the hero, and how it comes back, smaller, further down); and two or three saves that show the craft. Place those saves under the topic Gem.
- Test each idea: could it sit on another company's site? If yes, it is decoration, not a gem. Drop it and find another.
- When they pick one, make a study of it: a small sketch of the gem alone, large, on a plain ground. Not a page. This is where the effort goes: real craft, looked at and fixed until it is good. Give it knobs for everything worth tuning, and a seed knob if anything in it is random, so a look they like can be kept.
- If the gem is a family of pictures (a pattern, a set of illustrations, textures), the study is a maker: the designer tunes it with Knobs, points at the picture and presses Keep, and it goes into the project's brand assets. From then on use what they kept (its src from get_brand_assets), never a redrawing of it.
- If the gem is alive (a shader, an interaction), the study is the gem: later sketches take its code as it is, with the designer's knob values.

After the three ideas, ask which one. After the study, ask: go on to Color, or keep working on the gem?

Decision to write down: the gem in one sentence, and where it lives on the page.

### Color

Topic: color

Where color lives on the page, starting from the gem.

- If the project has brand colors (brand assets, a brand book), start from them. The question is then where they go, not what they are.
- One accent. Decide where it lives before you choose its exact value: the hero's ground? only the gem? only what can be pressed? Say why, from the gem and the words ("the stamp is red, so nothing else is").
- Then the rest: the ground, the ink, one or two quiet tones. Few. Not many shades and opacities of one color.
- Your own colors, not one of Panel's palettes, unless the designer asks for a palette.
- Show a color study: one sketch with the hero and two more sections from "Wireframe with words", small, with the colors in place and the gem where it goes. When there is a real choice (the accent as the ground, or the accent only in the gem), show both, each as its own sketch. Colors are knobs, each named by what it is for.
- Check that text can be read on every ground it sits on.

Ask which one, or what to change. Then: go on to Typography?

Decision to write down: each color as hex with where it goes, a line each.

### Typography

Topic: typography

The type, judged in the real words.

- The designer's own fonts first (get_fonts). Then fonts measured on sites they saved (search typography; measured_in_page says whether a font has Cyrillic and Tajik letters).
- Judge every font in this page's real words, in its language, at the sizes it will be used: the chosen headline, a paragraph, a button. Never by a Latin specimen.
- Two families at most. Decide the few sizes (headline, section heading, text, small) and keep to them.
- Show a type study: the hero's words and one more section, set for real on the chosen colors. When there is a real choice, show two pairings, each as its own sketch. Fonts and sizes are knobs.

Ask which one, or what to change. Then: go on to the hero?

Decision to write down: the families, weights and sizes, and what each is used for.

### Hero

The first screen, for real. Everything decided so far, together for the first time.

- One sketch, "Hero": the navbar and the hero only, with the chosen words, the gem at full craft, the colors and the type. From the topic Hero.
- This is where the feel is judged. Spend the effort here. Don't go on to the other sections yet.
- Look at it on a phone too (get_sketch with device phone) and fix what breaks.
- One direction, made well. It is the one they chose step by step, so don't offer alternates unless they ask.

Ask: does it feel right? Go on to Motion, or change something?

Decision to write down: what works, in the designer's words.

### Motion

Topic: motion

How it moves, on the hero they approved.

- Read the designer's motion skill first (get_skill: the one that leads "motion").
- Decide four things. How the page arrives: what appears first, second, third. How the gem moves, or answers the pointer. What hover and press do on everything that can be pressed. What scrolling does between sections.
- Each needs a reason: it explains something, it guides the eye, or it is the gem. Without a reason, it stays still.
- Add it to the hero as its next version (edit_sketch). Turn it down for people who ask for less motion (prefers-reduced-motion).
- Say what to look at and how ("reload to see it arrive; hover the button").

Ask: go on to the whole page, or change the motion?

Decision to write down: the four, a line each, with durations and easing.

### Whole page

The rest of the sections, built from everything decided.

- A new sketch from the hero, "Whole page": every section of the wireframe, in order, with its words.
- Each section follows what was decided: the colors where they were said to go, the type sizes, the motion. The gem comes back where the Gem step said it would.
- No section is a template block. For each one, ask what its job is, and what the plainest way is to do that job on this site. Three cards in a row is a choice, never a default.
- Real pictures from the project where there are any. Where a picture is still missing, a quiet gray block that says what it will be. Never a stock-looking stand-in.
- Links and buttons work. Checked on a phone.
- The last check, before you show it. Answer these in your reply, honestly:
  1. What is the one thing a visitor will remember? If you can't name it, it isn't done: go back to it.
  2. Could this page be another company's by changing the logo? Where?
  3. Which of the designer's rules does it brush against, and why is each of those still there?

There is no decision to lock. The designer chooses the sketch (Choose), and then it can be built.
