# HCI and design-cognition research on combining several design references into one new, coherent design

Notes compiled 16 November 2026. Every bullet carries a tag for the kind of claim:
**[M]** measured evidence (experiment, data set, logged behaviour) · **[Q]** qualitative observation from a study · **[T]** theory · **[P]** practice or opinion · **[S]** taken from a secondary source or abstract only, original not read in full.
Dates in brackets are the publication date of the source.

## 1. Research systems that recombine references with AI: how they split a reference, how they recombine, how much the human steers, what was measured

### Takeaway
Almost every tested system does the same three things: it splits each reference into named, typed parts the designer can see and edit (keywords, tokens, dimensions), lets the designer pick which parts to carry over, and makes several rough options from the picked parts instead of one finished picture. The measured gains are mostly in number of ideas, variety of prompts and self-reported creativity. Expert-rated quality or coherence of the mixed result is usually no better than the baseline; the one large, objective gain (VisiBlends, 96% against 21% successful blends) came from a narrow, explicit blending pattern that a human checks against two written criteria.

### Cited Findings

#### CreativeConnect (KAIST, CHI 2024) — the closest match to "mix parts of several references"
- [Q] Formative study, 6 early-career graphic designers: they take four kinds of element from a reference — subject matter, action and pose, theme and mood, arrangement (composition). Finding new ideas by combining elements was harder than pulling elements out; 3 of 6 worried about not having tried all combinations; 4 relied on imagining the mix, which often did not hold up once sketched; 4 preferred unfinished outputs (sketch or text) to avoid fixation and accidental copying. — [arXiv 2312.11949v2 (Dec 2023, rev. Mar 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11949v2)
- [M] Pipeline: (1) BLIP-2 captions the whole image and a 3×3 grid of crops; GPT-4 pulls keywords for subject, action, mood; Segment Anything gives the layout as boxes. (2) The designer picks keywords or adds their own; picked keywords sit next to their source picture on a movable mood board. (3) GPT-4 recommends related keywords. (4) Merge: the designer selects several keywords from different references; GPT-3.5 writes three one-line descriptions that join them and lists the objects to draw; a layout model places them (using a picked reference's arrangement, slightly varied, when the designer chose one); a diffusion model draws and a style-transfer step turns the result into a line sketch. Output is always a rough sketch plus a one-line caption. — [arXiv 2312.11949v2 (Mar 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11949v2)
- [M] Study: within-subjects, N=16 with design or art backgrounds, 30-minute sessions, a children's book cover from 10 references, against a baseline with manual keyword notes, the same image model and ChatGPT. Results (CreativeConnect vs baseline): keyword notes added 34.69 vs 13.19 (p<0.0001); helped discover elements 6.13 vs 3.75 (p=0.001); helped generate multiple ideas 5.94 vs 4.88 (p=0.023); sketches per session 5.56 vs 5.06 (p=0.041); self-rated creativity of sketches 5.38 vs 4.19 (p=0.004); the keywords merged in one input were less alike (minimum similarity 0.222 vs 0.263, p=0.008), i.e. people mixed more distant ideas. — [arXiv 2312.11949v2 (Mar 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11949v2)
- [M] What did not improve: expert-rated creativity 4.85 vs 4.34 (p=0.114) and expert-rated diversity 4.75 vs 4.63 (p=0.833) were not significant; workload no different; CSI was higher only for Expressiveness (p=0.032) and Collaboration (p=0.016). Action-and-pose keyword extraction had 35.3% precision. — [arXiv 2312.11949v2 (Mar 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11949v2)
- [Q] 12 of 16 preferred sketches to finished images because sparse sketches leave room to imagine and reduce fixation on a reference's details. CreativeConnect users credited the generated sketches and captions as inspiration and reinterpreted them; baseline users stayed tied to the original pictures. The tool suited people with no idea yet; the baseline suited people who already had a clear idea. Authors' stated limit: keywords are combined only through a text description; blending by object or by color was not tried. — [arXiv 2312.11949v2 (Mar 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11949v2)

#### AIdeation (National Taiwan University, CHI 2025) — recombining real reference images for concept art
- [Q] Formative study, 12 professional environment concept designers (3–15 years): they collect three kinds of reference — Hero/Main (overall mood and direction), Detailed Supporting (structure, texture), and Miscellaneous (lighting, atmosphere, palette, shot angle) — and usually make 3–5 variations per environment. Their complaints about image generators: outputs look alike across runs, details are unusable, hallucinations make outputs unsafe as references, and changing one thing changes the whole image. — [arXiv 2502.14747v1 (Feb 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1)
- [M] Pipeline: (1) text and/or image in; GPT-4o writes several ideas in parallel, each described in six parts — Theme, Contents (central focus / background), Art Style, Lighting and Atmosphere, Color Palette, Shot Angle — and each with a "creative score" from 0 to 1. (2) Each idea is split into keywords under those six headings and rendered with DALL-E 3. (3) Clicking a keyword runs a normal image search (50 real images per batch). (4) The designer picks one real reference for that keyword; the system captions it and rewrites only the matching part of the idea's description; the creative score sets how far the change spreads. (5) Each refinement gives 5 new designs, and the screen shows where each came from (for example idea 4 merged with a sofa reference). Composition was left out because the models could not control it. — [arXiv 2502.14747v1 (Feb 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1)
- [M] Study: within-subjects, 16 professional designers, 30-minute tasks, against each person's own workflow. 7-point preference scale tested against the midpoint: creativity mean 5.56 (p=0.001, 81% preferred AIdeation), breadth 5.19 (p=0.014), depth 5.00 (p=0.033), idea-generation efficiency 5.44 (p=0.003), satisfaction 5.19 (p=0.005). Idea quality 4.31 (p=0.41) — no better than baseline. All self-reported; no outside raters. — [arXiv 2502.14747v1 (Feb 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1)
- [Q] Field week in four studios (8 designers, 1,092 ideas, 60 selected, 12 environments): three studios reported less time (−25%, −60%, −57%), one more time (+25%, its client preferred another model's look); two studios kept using it. Views on control were split: some could steer a specific part, others could not keep an element (for example layout) fixed while changing another; one designer said they always got a similar style from AI images. — [arXiv 2502.14747v1 (Feb 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1)

#### VisiBlends (Columbia / Stanford, CHI 2019) — a blending pattern plus human checks
- [M] A visual blend is defined by four conditions: two concepts; one recognisable object for each; the two objects are joined into one object (side by side does not count); both stay recognisable. The pattern, "Single Shape Mapping": pick two objects that share one simple shape; map all of object A onto the matching part of object B; one object is then identified by its silhouette and the other by its color and inner detail. — [VisiBlends, CHI 2019 (May 2019)](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~chilton/web/my_publications/VisiBlends_CHI2019.pdf)
- [M] Six steps: people brainstorm associations for each concept; people find simple, iconic images; people mark each image's main shape and whether the shape covers all or part of the object; the system pairs images that have the same shape, an all-to-part match and aspect ratios within 50%; the system makes a mock-up; people judge each mock-up on two criteria (joined into one, both recognisable) and iterate. — [VisiBlends, CHI 2019](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~chilton/web/my_publications/VisiBlends_CHI2019.pdf)
- [M] Results: 13 novices made 5.67 successful blends per concept pair with the system against 0.55 without (p<0.001); success rate 96% against 21% (p<0.001); people who used it first fell back to 0.67 once it was taken away, so knowing the method alone was not enough. In a separate test the first pass found a blend for 11 of 16 random concept pairs. — [VisiBlends, CHI 2019](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~chilton/web/my_publications/VisiBlends_CHI2019.pdf)
- [Q] Without the system almost everyone was greedy: fix on one good image, then hope for a coincidence; when a blend failed they added more elements instead of starting again. Three kinds of useful iteration were seen: fix second-order features (find a version of the object whose color and style match the other), search for a missing shape when nothing matched, and handle constraints that only show up once the blend exists. People did their small step well only when they had been taught the whole workflow. — [VisiBlends, CHI 2019](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~chilton/web/my_publications/VisiBlends_CHI2019.pdf)

#### PopBlends (Columbia, CHI 2023) — find the shared idea first
- [M] A blend needs a connecting concept that both domains share (the example: "dirty" links a shampoo and a film scene). Stage 1 widens each domain into candidates and then selects concepts related to both; stage 2 finds a scene for each side and images for it. Three strategies (knowledge base only, half LLM, all LLM) gave about the same accuracy — 56.0%, 55.3%, 58.7% of connecting concepts judged related to both domains — but different kinds of suggestion; at least one good concept among five came up 93.3% of the time. — [arXiv 2111.04920 (Nov 2021, rev. Feb 2023)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.04920)
- [M] Study: 10 amateur designers, within-subjects, 5 minutes per pair, against web search: 2.03 vs 0.87 blend ideas per pair (p<0.001); mental demand 2.85 vs 5.80 (p=0.004). The system suggests image pairs; it does not make the final blend. — [arXiv 2111.04920](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.04920)

#### Luminate (CHI 2024) — a design space made of dimensions (text domain)
- [M] From one prompt the LLM first writes dimensions (up to 5 nominal with up to 8 values, up to 3 ordinal with 5 levels), then builds each of 40 responses by drawing one value per dimension; responses are shown as dots that can be arranged along one or two dimensions, zoomed from dot to title to summary to full text, filtered, extended ("More like this"), and the user can add a dimension of their own. — [arXiv 2310.12953v3 (Oct 2023, rev. Mar 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12953v3)
- [M] Study: 14 professional writers, no baseline condition. CSI 82.16 (SD 5.14); all agreed the dimensions helped them think about the factors of the task; 10 of 14 preferred it to ChatGPT; some found 40 responses too many. Authors claim (not test) that it can prevent fixation. — [arXiv 2310.12953v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12953v3)

#### DesignWeaver (UC San Diego, CHI 2025) — a palette of dimensions and tags read off generated images
- [Q] Formative, 12 product designers: clients cannot put preferences into words and react to pictures; designers show several alternatives, often three, to bring out priorities and trade-offs. — [arXiv 2502.09867v2 (Feb 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09867v2)
- [M] Pipeline: GPT-4o pulls three starting dimensions and tags from the brief; the designer clicks tags in a palette (dimensions as rows); GPT-4o rewrites the prompt from the selected tags while keeping its structure; DALL-E 3 gives three images; a vision model reads new tags and dimensions off those images and adds them to the palette. — [arXiv 2502.09867v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09867v2)
- [M] Study: between-subjects, 52 novices, a dining chair from a brief, against a plain prompt box. Prompts 48.2 vs 23.7 words (p<0.001); unique design terms 34.6 vs 16.2 (p<0.001); generated images less alike (CLIP similarity 0.863 vs 0.903, p<0.001); expert-rated novelty 4.09 vs 3.54 (p=0.002). Expert-rated fit to the brief 4.5 vs 4.2 (p=0.059) and satisfaction (5.59 vs 5.56) did not differ. 5 of 27 found too many tags confusing; people could not get specific details (a curved back, an exact color); richer prompts raised expectations the image model could not meet. — [arXiv 2502.09867v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09867v2)

#### Misty (Apple, CHI 2025) — blending a UI example into your own UI code
- [M] Built on conceptual blending: the developer drops a screenshot or sketch of an example onto their work-in-progress screen (whole screen, or one region onto one area) and chooses which aspect to take — Color, Layout, Content, or a custom instruction; GPT-4o rewrites the React code so the developer's own content is kept. Each result comes with a "semantic diff": the changes grouped by meaning (for example switched to a dark theme), each group a switch that can be turned off. — [arXiv 2409.13900v3 (Sep 2024; CHI Apr 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13900v3)
- [M] First-use study, 14 frontend developers, 103 blends: mean satisfaction 3.2 of 5; 11 of 14 found it easy; 9 said it helped explore alternatives; only 5 agreed it let them express intent well and only 4 said outputs generally matched expectations. Failures: ignored instructions, poor alignment and sizing, a style applied to one button but not the others. Chance results were welcome early and unwelcome once the person had a specific design in mind; people wanted a dial for this. — [arXiv 2409.13900v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13900v3)

#### Inkspire (CMU / Toyota Research Institute, CHI 2025) — analogies and sketch scaffolds
- [M] The designer names a subject and an abstract quality (a "protective" car); GPT-4 first states design principles for the subject, then offers 10 concrete analogies from nature, architecture or fashion (tortoise, armour). The designer sketches; every stroke regenerates a design; the generated design is turned back into a faint line underlay to sketch over. Early strokes are read loosely, later ones tightly. — [arXiv 2501.18588v1 (Jan 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18588v1)
- [M] Study: within-subjects, 12 (6 professional product designers), against ControlNet with a prompt box. Exploration 5.83 vs 3.83, inspiration 5.92 vs 4.00, controllability 5.58 vs 4.17, partnership 5.83 vs 3.42 (all p<0.01); prompts within a session were less alike (0.51 vs 0.76). Final design quality 5.92 vs 4.58 was not significant. Baseline users kept one prompt and made small edits. — [arXiv 2501.18588v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18588v1)

#### Artinter (University of Michigan, DIS 2023) — user-named concepts with weights
- [M] Artist and client group images on a shared mood board and give each group their own name ("rough brush", "flat"); linking two concepts as contrasts sharpens the model; the system learns each concept from the grouped examples. Search then has one slider per concept (less … more) from a chosen starting image. Generation mixes styles of chosen images or concepts with a weight and a scale for each, and a crop to use only part of a reference. — [Artinter, DIS 2023 (Jul 2023)](https://johnr0.github.io/assets/publications/DIS2023-Artinter.pdf)
- [Q] Study 1, six artist–client pairs: people reached what they wanted by changing weights step by step, and the differences between successive results showed them what they actually wanted; every session used generated results as a starting point to talk over or sketch on, not as the answer. A failure: an artist expected flat color from the style reference and got mixed gradients — the person's idea of what "mixing" does did not match the model's. One artist was frightened by how well it mimicked their style. — [Artinter, DIS 2023](https://johnr0.github.io/assets/publications/DIS2023-Artinter.pdf)

#### DesignPrompt, FusAIn, DesignTrace (Université Paris-Saclay, DIS 2024 / CHI 2025 / CHI 2026)
- [S] DesignPrompt: a mood board tool where a prompt is built from images, colors and semantic tags; the tool shows both the mixed input and the text prompt it was translated into, and the designer can edit and reorder it. Structured observation with 12 professional designers against text-only prompting: mixed input helped them explore and express intent, and designers invented a prompt pattern meant to get the most novelty while keeping consistency. Numbers not retrieved. — [HAL record hal-04652020 (DIS, Jul 2024)](https://hal.u-pec.fr/LISN-EX-SITU/hal-04652020v1)
- [P] The authors' later summary: designers hold some attributes steady (for example a color palette) while exploring variation in others; FusAIn loads "pens" with a texture, color or object taken from a reference image and the designer draws with them, so only that property is applied where the pen goes; DesignTrace takes semantic attributes from inspiration images, applies them to a region or the whole image, and keeps branches of alternatives as nodes that can be revisited. The paper also notes that repeated generations drift away from the original intent. No user-study numbers in this source. — [arXiv 2603.03074 (Mar 2026, CHI 2026 workshop)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03074v1)

#### Other systems, 2022–2026
- [S] GenQuery (KAIST, CHI 2024): makes a vague search query concrete, lets the designer change part of a found image with another image or with keywords from their search history, then searches with the changed image. 16 designers felt they could express intent more exactly and found more satisfying results than with a tool without generation. — [arXiv 2310.01287v2 (Oct 2023, rev. Mar 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01287v2)
- [S] MoodCubes (Autodesk Research, DIS 2022): a 3D mood board that breaks added items into parts (for example pulls out color palettes), suggests related material and offers filters to change the whole scene's look; studied with 8 creative professionals. — [Autodesk Research (Jun 2022)](https://research.autodesk.com/publications/moodcubes-immersive-spaces-for-collecting-discovering-and-envisioning-inspiration-materials/)
- [S] Brickify (CHI 2025): turns subject, style and color taken from reference images into reusable "design tokens" that the designer resizes, groups and links to say what to use and how the parts go together; designers found it more efficient and intuitive than text prompts. No numbers in the abstract. — [arXiv 2502.21219 (Feb 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.21219)
- [M] IdeaBlocks (KAIST; arXiv Jul 2025, v4 May 2026): an exploration intent has three parts — property, direction, range — and is packed into a reusable block (reused within a block, a path or a project). Against a baseline (N=12) people explored 2.13 times more images with 12.5% greater visual diversity; a 3-day deployment (N=6) followed. — [arXiv 2507.22163 (May 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22163)
- [S] Expandora (CHI 2025 late-breaking): the designer states what to explore and how much diversity they want; an LLM writes prompt variations shown as a mind map. N=8: more varied prompts, more prompts tried, higher satisfaction; weak support for narrowing down. — [arXiv 2503.00791 (Mar 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00791)
- [M] Brief2Design (arXiv Apr 2026, preprint): interviews with 6 professional graphic designers gave three phases — structure the vague brief, explore each element separately (objects, background, text, typography, composition), recombine the chosen alternatives. Against a chat baseline (N=12): more varied prompts and high ratings for pulling requirements out of the brief, but it took longer and the images were no more varied. — [arXiv 2604.11019 (Apr 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11019)
- [S] ToMigo (arXiv Feb 2026): reads reference images and text into a "design concept graph" — nodes are choices of purpose, content or style, edges explain how they connect — which the designer edits; users reported more control and high alignment. No numbers in the abstract. — [arXiv 2602.05825 (Feb 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05825)
- [S] Protosampling / Atelier (Autodesk Research, arXiv Jan 2026): a canvas that keeps collected references and generated pictures in one space; no study results in the abstract. — [arXiv 2601.05401 (Jan 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05401)
- [S] DesignAID (MIT / Toyota Research Institute, Collective Intelligence 2023): an LLM writes deliberately diverse ideas, an image model draws them for a mood board; designers rated it more inspiring and useful than image search; one summary says the high-diversity setting was only slightly better for inspiration and no better otherwise. — [MIT DSpace (2023)](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/153130); [MIT Sloan write-up](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/a-generative-ai-tool-to-inspire-creative-workers)

### Inferences
- The field has settled on "typed parts, human picks, several rough outputs". None of the systems lets the AI decide the mix alone, and the ones tested with professionals (AIdeation, CreativeConnect's formative work) keep real reference images in the loop rather than only model-made ones.
- No paper found here measures the coherence of a mix directly. The usual measures are counts, diversity, questionnaires and occasionally expert novelty. So "AI mixes are incoherent" is not something this literature has solved; it has mostly made exploring more mixes cheaper.
- The only system with a large objective success gain (VisiBlends) differs from the rest in two ways: the combination follows an explicit structural rule about what must be shared and what each source contributes, and a human judges each result against two written criteria. That points to "a stated rule for how the parts fit" as the missing piece in looser keyword-merge tools.
- Text is the usual meeting point for the parts (keywords → description → image). CreativeConnect's authors name this as a limit. Misty and AIdeation get more control by rewriting only one part of an existing description or code base, which keeps everything else still.
- Provenance views (AIdeation's origin label, Misty's semantic diff, ToMigo's graph) are the tested ways to let the designer see and undo what came from where.

### Gaps
- DesignPrompt's numbers and FusAIn's study results could not be read (the HAL page blocked automated access; FusAIn's paper was not found in open form). Only the authors' own later summary was available.
- Artinter's second study (20 participants, with and without AI features) was not read to its results.
- For Brickify, ToMigo, Expandora and Protosampling only abstracts were read.
- No 2025–2026 system was found that mixes UI or web references facet by facet (color from one, type from another, motion from a third) and has outside raters judge coherence. Misty is the nearest, and it measured satisfaction, not coherence.
- No tested system was found for motion references.

## 2. Design fixation and examples: what makes combining examples work or fail in people

### Takeaway
Examples help novelty and quality but narrow variety, and people copy features of what they are shown, flaws included, even when told not to; AI image generators make this worse, not better. What helps, on the evidence: few and uncommon examples, seen early and when the person asks; turning an example into a stated principle before reuse, best done by comparing two examples; and treating "far" sources with care — they raise novelty in lab tasks, but in real design work the better ideas built on nearer sources.

### Cited Findings
- [S][M] Jansson and Smith (1991, Design Studies 12(1), 3–11): designers shown a picture of an existing solution repeated its features more than a control group, including features that broke the brief, and did so even when told to avoid them; a fourth study showed the effect in professionals. Purcell and Gero (1996) found it in mechanical engineers but not clearly in other groups. — [secondary review (core.ac.uk)](https://core.ac.uk/download/237398528.pdf); [Design Society paper](https://cognitive.designsociety.org/download-publication/39819/Studying+design+fixation+with+a+computer-based+task)
- [M] Sio, Kotovsky and Cagan (2015, Design Studies 39, 70–99), meta-analysis of 43 design studies: examples made people produce more example-related ideas and fewer categories of ideas, but the ideas were more novel and of higher quality; the benefit grew with fewer and less common examples — one single, uncommon example helped most; quality rose with the degree of copying from the example. (Abstract read only.) — [DOI 10.1016/j.destud.2015.04.004 (Jul 2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.destud.2015.04.004)
- [M] Wadinambiarachchi et al. (CHI 2024), N=60, 20 minutes sketching chatbot avatars after seeing one example: fixation on the example was higher with an AI image generator (+0.32, Bayes factor 124) and with image search (+0.27) than with no support; neither improved number, variety or originality of ideas. Prompts averaged 3.5 words; 44% reused words from the brief and 44% of AI images resembled the example; sketch fixation tracked the fixation of the AI images just seen (ρ=0.56). People moved their fixation from the example onto the AI's outputs ("fixation displacement"). — [arXiv 2403.11164 (Mar 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11164v1)
- [P] The same authors recommend: help the person reinterpret the brief and mix unrelated ideas into prompts; remind them to consider alternatives; generate abstract, partial or blurred images instead of detailed ones; help them find abstract correspondences between an image and the problem. Not tested in that paper. — [arXiv 2403.11164 (Mar 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11164v1)
- [M] Chan et al. (2011, Journal of Mechanical Design 133): far-field and less common examples raised the novelty of solutions (and the spread of their quality); far-field plus less common beat the no-example control on novelty; pictures and text worked alike. (Abstract read only.) — [paper PDF (Aug 2011)](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/Chanetal2011.pdf)
- [M] Chan, Dow and Schunn (2015, Design Studies 36, 31–58), real data from the OpenIDEO platform: citing sources of inspiration went with more creative ideas, but conceptually nearer sources, not farther ones, went with ideas the expert panel shortlisted. — [author manuscript (2014/2015)](https://joelchan.github.io/files/2014-desstudies-nearfar-in-press.pdf); [CMU summary (Nov 2014)](https://www.hcii.cmu.edu/news/think-outside-box-not-so-fast)
- [M] Siangliulue et al. (Creativity & Cognition 2015), N=97 online: people who asked for examples themselves produced the ideas rated most novel; examples shown when the person went idle produced the most ideas; examples pushed at a fixed interval produced fewer ideas than no examples at all. — [paper PDF (Jun 2015)](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/siangliulue15providing.pdf)
- [M] Kulkarni, Dow and Klemmer (CogSci 2012), N=81 drawing task: examples seen early improved creativity; examples repeated between rounds of making did better still; examples seen late increased conformity without improving creativity. — [Stanford HCI record (2012)](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/paper.php?id=206)
- [S][M] Gick and Holyoak (1983, Cognitive Psychology 15, 1–38): given two prior analogs, people often formed a general schema while describing what the two had in common, and the schema helped transfer; a single example gave little transfer. Gentner, Loewenstein and Thompson (2003, Journal of Educational Psychology 95, 393–408): people who compared two cases side by side used the underlying principle later more than people who studied the same cases one at a time. — [Gick & Holyoak record](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/items/196ea13e-fb69-4a74-a6f9-864c81aed465/full); [Gentner et al. PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/learning-and-transfer-a-general-role-for-analogical-encoding-1j5d5qfzya.pdf)
- [S][M] Yu, Kittur and Kraut (CHI 2014, two papers): splitting idea generation into "write the schema of an example" then "apply the schema to a new domain" gave better ideas than working from raw examples across three experiments; schema quality mattered (expert-written schemas gave the best ideas); people given an abstracted version of a problem found more analogs that differed on the surface. (Abstract and class summaries only.) — [bibliographic record, DOI 10.1145/2556288.2557371](https://bibtex.github.io/CHI-2014-YuKK14a.html); [class summary](https://wordpress.cs.vt.edu/cs6724f15/?p=117)
- [M] Hope, Chan, Kittur and Shahaf (KDD 2017): describing each product only by its purpose and its mechanism (a deliberately weak structure) found analogies with better precision and recall than plain text similarity, and the analogies it returned raised people's chance of producing a creative idea. — [arXiv 1706.05585 (Jun 2017)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1706.05585)
- [M] VisiBlends: the shared abstract structure (one shape) is what made combination tractable; novices without it made 0.55 successful blends per pair, with it 5.67. Its authors argue that to find such a pattern one must ignore surface detail and look for what matters to perception. — [VisiBlends, CHI 2019](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~chilton/web/my_publications/VisiBlends_CHI2019.pdf)
- [M] Kumar et al. (CHI 2025), two pre-registered experiments, 1,100 participants: LLM help raised performance while it was available, but people who had it did worse afterwards on their own than people who never had it; on divergent tasks LLM exposure did not help originality. — [arXiv 2410.03703 (Sep 2024, rev. Feb 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03703v2)

### Inferences
- "Show the model three references and ask for a mix" is the fixation set-up: finished, detailed examples, no stated principle, nothing saying what to leave behind. People in that position copy surface features; there is no reason to expect a model given only pixels and captions to do better.
- The schema evidence suggests a concrete step: before a reference's part is reused, state it as a principle (what it does and why it works there), ideally by comparing two references that share it. The principle travels; the surface look stays behind.
- Near and far sources seem to do different jobs. Near sources (same kind of product) carry structure that works; far sources (a poster for a website, a motion piece for a static page) carry novelty. The lab and field results stop conflicting if far sources are used for a small share of the design.
- Timing and number matter: a few uncommon references, early and on request, beat many references pushed automatically.

### Gaps
- Jansson and Smith (1991) was not read in the original; the summary is from secondary reviews.
- Sio et al. (2015) effect sizes per moderator, and Chan et al. (2011) statistics, were not read (abstracts only).
- Yu, Kittur and Kraut's exact effect sizes were not retrieved; Kittur et al. (2019, PNAS, "Scaling up analogical innovation with crowds and AI") was confirmed to exist as a perspective piece but not read.
- No study was found that tests schema-first reuse specifically for visual style (color, type, layout) rather than product ideas.
- No study was found on how many references a person can combine before coherence breaks.

## 3. Blending theory: what makes a blend good

### Takeaway
Blending theory says a blend is not an average of its inputs: the inputs must share some structure, only selected parts of each are carried over, and the result must hold together as one thing with properties of its own. The easiest blends to get right take their organising frame from one input and fill it with material from the others; blends that draw frame from two inputs are the most inventive and the hardest. The empirical work on visual blends agrees: fused, simple, both-sources-recognisable blends are liked most.

### Cited Findings
- [T] Fauconnier and Turner ("Conceptual Integration Networks", Cognitive Science 22(2), 133–187, 1998; The Way We Think, 2002): a blend has two or more input spaces, a generic space holding what the inputs share, cross-space mappings between counterparts, and a blended space that receives selected elements from each input. Three operations give the blend structure that is in neither input: composition (relations that appear only when the parts are put together), completion (background knowledge fills in the pattern) and elaboration (running the blend as its own thing). — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_blending); [bibliographic page for the 1998 paper](https://markturner.org/cin.web/cin.html)
- [T] Four kinds of network: simplex (one input is a frame, the other fills its slots), mirror (all inputs share one frame), single-scope (inputs have different frames, only one frame organises the blend), double-scope (parts of both frames organise the blend). — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_blending)
- [S][T] Governing (optimality) principles from The Way We Think, as summarised in linguistics papers: Integration (the blend should be one well-integrated whole), Topology (relations among elements in the inputs should be kept in the blend where possible), Web (the blend should stay connected to its inputs), Unpacking (from the blend alone one should be able to rebuild the inputs and their links), Relevance (every element in the blend should have a reason to be there), plus compression of relations. The principles pull against each other and a good blend balances them. — [secondary summary (Schmid)](https://www.anglistik.uni-muenchen.de/personen/professoren/schmid/schmid_publ/blending_relevance.pdf); [secondary summary (Ruiz de Mendoza)](https://www.italian-journal-linguistics.com/app/uploads/2021/06/04.Ruiz-De-Mendoza.pdf)
- [T] Criticism: Gibbs (2000) argued the theory lacks testable hypotheses and works better as a framework; Ritchie (2004) argued simpler processes explain many blends. — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_blending)
- [S][T] Koestler (The Act of Creation, 1964): creative acts join two normally separate frames of thought ("bisociation of matrices"); cited as the forerunner of blending theory. Boden: three kinds of creativity — combinational (unfamiliar combinations of familiar ideas), exploratory (moving within a style's rules) and transformational (changing the rules); she held that bisociation alone does not explain transformational ideas. — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_blending); [secondary essay on Boden (Aug 2025)](https://www.themarginalian.org/2025/08/22/margaret-boden-creativity/); [paper on computational bisociation](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstreams/42f05140-364b-4bfc-bfbc-03cc56e556f3/download)
- [M] Visual blends: VisiBlends' working definition matches Integration and Unpacking — the two objects must be joined into one object, and both must stay recognisable. Its pattern assigns each source a different job (one gives the silhouette, the other the color and detail) on top of one shared shape. Novices who placed two good symbols side by side, or pasted one on top of the other, did not produce a blend. — [VisiBlends, CHI 2019](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~chilton/web/my_publications/VisiBlends_CHI2019.pdf)
- [S][M] Studies of visual metaphor in advertising, as reported in VisiBlends: fused "hybrid" images had the most positive effect on viewers compared with side-by-side or implied ones; high complexity went with lower appreciation, and simpler blends with fewer objects were easier to read (Forceville; van Mulken, le Pair and Forceville 2010, Journal of Pragmatics 42(12)). — [VisiBlends related work, CHI 2019](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~chilton/web/my_publications/VisiBlends_CHI2019.pdf)
- [M] PopBlends puts the generic space first: it searches for a concept both inputs share before it looks for anything to show. With that step, people found 2.03 blend ideas per pair against 0.87 without. — [arXiv 2111.04920](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.04920)
- [M] Misty applies selective projection to interfaces: the designer chooses which aspect of the example (color, layout, content) is projected onto their own screen, and their own content stays. A common failure was partial projection — a style reaching one button and not its siblings. — [arXiv 2409.13900v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13900v3)
- [M] Unity and variety: Post, Blijlevens and Hekkert (2016, Acta Psychologica 163, 142–152), three experiments on product designs: unity and variety each raise aesthetic appreciation while working against each other; designs with the best balance are preferred; unity is the dominant factor and makes variety easier to appreciate. (Abstract read only.) — [TU Delft record (2016)](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/to-preserve-unity-while-almost-allowing-for-chaos-testing-the-aes/)

### Inferences
- "Color from one, shapes from another, a detail from a third" is, in these terms, selective projection from three inputs. Theory and the visual-blend studies agree that it works when (a) there is a stated shared ground, (b) one input supplies the organising frame, (c) each other input supplies a different, non-competing kind of feature, and (d) the result is checked as one whole.
- A single-scope blend is the safe default for a tool: one reference sets the frame (structure, layout logic, overall mood), the others fill slots (palette, type, a motif, a motion idea). Double-scope blends are where new structure comes from, but they need more checking and more iteration.
- The governing principles can be turned into review questions: Is it one thing? (Integration.) Do the relations that made each borrowed part work in its source still hold here — for example a palette's contrast roles? (Topology.) Can you still tell what came from where? (Unpacking, Web.) Does every borrowed part earn its place? (Relevance.)
- Emergent structure explains why a mix has to be rendered and looked at: some constraints only show up once the parts are together (VisiBlends' emergent constraints; CreativeConnect's designers whose imagined mixes failed on paper).

### Gaps
- The Way We Think was not read directly; the list of governing principles comes from secondary papers and should be checked against the book before being quoted.
- Boden's and Koestler's own texts were not read; the summaries are secondary.
- No experiment was found that tests the single-scope versus double-scope distinction on interface, web or graphic design outcomes.
- Blending theory describes meaning, not pixels; its use for visual style is by analogy (as VisiBlends and Misty do).

## 4. Established methods for turning several references into one direction

### Takeaway
Two families of method apply. Morphological analysis lists the dimensions of a design, lists the options for each (here, one per reference), and then checks every pair of options for whether they can live together before any whole combination is considered — a cheap way to throw out incoherent mixes. Designers' practice methods (style tiles, element collages, stylescapes, mood board with a hero image) all work on layout-free fragments first and settle one dominant idea with supporting parts; these are practice, not tested evidence, apart from the interview studies that describe them.

### Cited Findings
- [P][T] General morphological analysis (Zwicky; Ritchey 2002, revised 2013): (1) name the parameters of the problem; (2) give each a set of possible values; (3) put them in a grid (the "Zwicky box") — every pick of one value per parameter is a configuration, so the count multiplies quickly (the article's example 4×4×4×3×3×4 = 2,304); (4) cross-consistency assessment: compare every pair of values and judge only whether the two can coexist, not which causes which; any configuration that contains a contradictory pair is dropped. — [Ritchey, Swedish Morphological Society (2002, rev. 2013)](https://www.swemorph.com/ma.html)
- [P][T] Three kinds of inconsistency: logical (the two contradict by definition), empirical (judged very unlikely on evidence) and normative (ruled out on grounds of values). Logical and empirical judgments come first; what is desirable is judged only after what is possible. The pairwise work is small next to the space it prunes: a field of about 100,000 configurations needs only a few hundred pair judgments. — [Ritchey (2002, rev. 2013)](https://www.swemorph.com/ma.html)
- [P][T] Once pruned, the grid is an "inference model": fix one or more values as given and see which values of the other parameters remain consistent with them. — [Ritchey (2002, rev. 2013)](https://www.swemorph.com/ma.html)
- [M] Luminate, DesignWeaver and Brief2Design are AI versions of the grid without the consistency check: an LLM writes the dimensions and values, and outputs are built by drawing one value per dimension (Luminate draws them at random). — [Luminate, arXiv 2310.12953v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12953v3); [DesignWeaver, arXiv 2502.09867v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09867v2); [Brief2Design, arXiv 2604.11019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11019)
- [P] Style tiles (Samantha Warren): a deliverable of fonts, colors and interface elements that sits between a mood board (too vague) and a full comp (too literal) and deliberately leaves out layout. Process: listen; interpret — gather the client's adjectives, group them into themes, turn themes into design principles; define a visual language by matching themes to styles; iterate without layout. Several tiles are shown, and mixing elements across tiles is expected as the way the direction is refined. — [styletil.es (site current 2022–2026)](https://styletil.es/); [attribution to Warren, course page (2021)](https://openlab.bmcc.cuny.edu/mmp-350-fall-21/?p=1666)
- [P] Element collages (Dan Mall, 2012; described by Cloud Four, 2016): after mood boards, each designer makes a collage of designed fragments without a full layout. Cloud Four's account: one collage each with notes on color, imagery and type; a survey so every voice counts; name each collage's strongest part; second round, each designer borrows what worked from the others; the final direction was one designer's collage carrying a second designer's palette and a third's hero treatment, chosen because it felt most like the brand. — [Cloud Four (3 Mar 2016)](https://cloudfour.com/thinks/element-collages-are-fun/)
- [P][S] Stylescapes: a composed board, between mood board and mock-up, showing color, type and imagery working together as one proposed direction, used to agree direction before detailed design. — [agency explainer (Jul 2026)](https://scyllagroup.com/insights/2026/what-is-a-stylescape/); [practitioner newsletter](https://weeklysop.substack.com/p/stylescapes-visual-direction-before)
- [Q] How professionals sort references (AIdeation's 12 concept designers): a Hero/Main reference sets overall mood and direction; Detailed Supporting references give structure and texture; Miscellaneous references give lighting, atmosphere, palette and angle. They explore by sketching many combinations — one said they often fill a whole A3 sheet. — [arXiv 2502.14747v1 (Feb 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1)
- [Q] How graphic designers recombine (CreativeConnect's 6 designers): four element types taken from references; recombination happens mostly at the concept stage, before style detail. Brief2Design's 6 professionals: structure the brief, explore elements one at a time, recombine chosen alternatives. DesignWeaver's 12 product designers: show several alternatives, often three. — [arXiv 2312.11949v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11949v2); [arXiv 2604.11019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11019); [arXiv 2502.09867v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09867v2)
- [P] Dominance (Steven Bradley, Smashing Magazine): a design should have one dominant element as the way in; three levels are the most people can tell apart — dominant (most visual weight, usually one element), sub-dominant (the other focal points, secondary emphasis) and subordinate (least weight, usually body text); two elements competing for dominance distract; emphasising everything emphasises nothing. — [Smashing Magazine (27 Feb 2015)](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2015/02/design-principles-dominance-focal-points-hierarchy/)
- [P][S] Mood board guides from agencies and tool vendors (low evidential weight; individual attribution not checked): make one or two "hero" images visibly larger as the anchor; let supporting images fill out the anchor's direction rather than compete; group color and texture references so the color story reads as one; a board mixing a warm-toned and a cool-toned reference is giving two directions; start from a one-sentence statement of the brief. — [example guide](https://www.relevantaudience.com/web-design/what-is-a-mood-board/); [example guide](https://www.artfolio.com/article/mood-boards-that-translate-abstract-concepts-into-actionable-photo-shoot-guides)

### Inferences
- Cross-consistency assessment fits the mixing problem closely. With references as the source of values (palette from A or B, type from B or C, layout logic from A or D, motion from E), the tool can ask for each pair "can these two live together, and if not, why?" before generating anything. This is the step the AI systems in section 1 skip, and skipping it is a plausible reason loose keyword merges come out incoherent. The "why" for each rejected pair is useful to show the designer.
- The inference-model idea maps to locking: once the designer fixes one choice (this palette), the tool shows which remaining options still fit.
- Practice methods agree on working with fragments that carry no layout (tile, collage) before a full page. That matches the measured preference for low-fidelity outputs in section 1.
- Practice methods and the interview studies agree on roles: one hero or dominant source, supporting sources, small accents. This is the single-scope blend of section 3 in designers' own words.
- Cloud Four's two-round process (make separately, name each one's strongest part, borrow, then one person consolidates) is a human recipe for recombination in which one author makes the final whole.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source was found for the 60-30-10 rule's origin or for any test of it; searches returned only unsourced lifestyle and vendor pages. Treat it as a rule of thumb with no evidence found.
- Dan Mall's original 2012 post (danielmall.com/articles/rif-element-collages/) could not be opened; the description here is from Cloud Four.
- The link between stylescapes and any named originator could not be confirmed.
- No study was found that tests cross-consistency assessment, style tiles, element collages or stylescapes for their effect on design quality.
- No published first-hand account by a named art director of how they merge several references into one direction was found in the searches run; what was found is agency and vendor guidance.

## 5. Novelty with familiarity: what balance works and how a tool could aim for it

### Takeaway
The steady finding across product design, science and music is that the best-received work is mostly conventional with a small share of something unusual — and that both typicality and novelty help once you hold the other still. For a tool, that means a familiar, proven base (structure, patterns the audience knows) with one or two atypical injections from more distant references, and unity kept as the first priority.

### Cited Findings
- [M] Hekkert, Snelders and van Wieringen (2003, British Journal of Psychology 94, 111–124), three studies on consumer products: typicality and novelty are about equally strong predictors of aesthetic preference but suppress each other; people prefer novel designs so long as the novelty does not cost typicality; together the two explained about 56% of the variance in preference. Tested Loewy's "most advanced, yet acceptable". — [summary with figures](https://www.jimdavies.org/summaries/HekkertSneldersVanwieringen2003.html); [VU record (2003)](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/most-advanced-yet-acceptable-typicality-and-novelty-as-joint-pred/)
- [M] Caveat: an apparel study found the principle did not hold across all clothing categories. — [ITAA proceedings](https://iastatedigitalpress.com/itaa/article/1593/galley/1466/view)
- [M] Uzzi, Mukherjee, Stringer and Jones (2013, Science 342, 468–472), 17.9 million papers: each paper was scored by how conventional its typical pairing of sources is (median) and how unusual its most unusual pairings are (10th percentile). Papers high on both — a very conventional core plus a tail of unusual pairings — were hits (top 5% by citations) 9.11% of the time, nearly twice the 5% base rate; conventional core without the unusual tail 5.82%; unusual tail without the conventional core 5.33%; neither 2.05%. Only 6.7% of papers were in the winning group. Teams were 37.7% more likely than solo authors to add the unusual tail. — [Science (25 Oct 2013)](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/uzzi/htm/papers/Science-2013-Uzzi-468-72.pdf)
- [M] Askin and Mauskapf (2017, American Sociological Review), about 27,000 Billboard Hot 100 songs: songs that sound too much like their peers do worse; the best performers are "optimally differentiated" — somewhat, not wildly, different. — [Columbia Business School record (2017)](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/what-makes-popular-culture-popular-product-features-and-optimal-differentiation)
- [M] Berger and Packard (2018, Psychological Science): the more a song's lyrics differ from its genre's usual lyrics, the more popular it was. — [APS summary (2018)](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/obsonline/to-climb-the-charts-write-lyrics-that-stand-out.html)
- [T][M] Berlyne: novelty and complexity raise arousal, and liking is highest at a middle level (inverted U). Evidence is mixed: Marin et al. (2016) found a strong straight-line link from complexity to arousal (r > 0.85), and an inverted U for beauty only after controlling for familiarity; other measures of liking behaved differently. A review of music-preference studies judged 50 of 57 compatible with an inverted-U model. — [Marin et al., Frontiers in Human Neuroscience (2016)](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2016.00536/full); [review listing (date not verified)](https://scite.ai/authors/anthony-chmiel-DKepz?page=2)
- [M] Post, Blijlevens and Hekkert (2016): unity and variety both raise appreciation while opposing each other; unity dominates and makes variety easier to like. — [TU Delft record (2016)](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/to-preserve-unity-while-almost-allowing-for-chaos-testing-the-aes/)
- [M] From section 2: one single, uncommon example helps most (Sio et al. 2015); far and uncommon examples raise novelty in the lab (Chan et al. 2011); nearer sources went with the shortlisted ideas in real work (Chan, Dow and Schunn 2015). — [Sio et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.destud.2015.04.004); [Chan et al. 2011](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/Chanetal2011.pdf); [Chan, Dow & Schunn](https://joelchan.github.io/files/2014-desstudies-nearfar-in-press.pdf)
- [Q][M] How tools have aimed for the balance: AIdeation's per-idea "creative score" (0–1) sets how far a reference changes the design; DesignPrompt's designers held a palette steady while varying other inputs to get novelty with consistency; Misty's users wanted chance early and determinism late; Expandora asks for a desired diversity level. — [AIdeation](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1); [DesignPrompt summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03074v1); [Misty](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13900v3); [Expandora](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00791)
- [M] In a text study, outputs pushed away from consensus phrasing were rated more useful and more coherent, and originality and coherence rose together across systems (ρ +0.40 to +0.67), so more divergence did not have to cost coherence. Preprint, 16 participants. — [arXiv 2606.09587 (Jun 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09587)

### Inferences
- A workable target for a mixed design: the main mass conventional (a frame and patterns the audience already reads easily, taken from a near reference), plus a small tail of atypical combination (one or two parts from a far reference). Uzzi's result says the pairing matters — novelty alone and convention alone both do about as well as the base rate.
- The library itself gives a way to measure this: "typical" can be defined against the designer's own references of the same kind (how close is this palette or layout to the usual one for this kind of page), and "novel" as distance from them. A tool could report both, and warn when a mix is typical on every dimension or atypical on every dimension.
- Since unity dominates variety, the atypical part should go where it does not break the frame: one dimension at a time (color or type or motion), with the rest held.
- A "how far" control per idea, loose early and tight late, is the tested interface pattern for this.

### Gaps
- No study found gives a numeric ratio of conventional to novel for visual design; "mostly conventional with a small unusual tail" is the most the evidence supports. Uzzi's figures are for scientific papers.
- Hekkert et al. (2003) was read through a summary, not the paper; Askin and Mauskapf and Berger and Packard through abstracts and press summaries.
- "Optimal distinctiveness" as a named theory (strategy and sociology literature) was not searched beyond the music study.
- No study was found on whether novelty that comes from a cross-reference mix is received differently from novelty that comes from a single unusual choice.

## 6. Homogenization risk when designers use AI for ideation, and what keeps variety

### Takeaway
Across controlled studies, people working with generative AI produce work that is individually rated as good or better but more alike across people, and AI image tools raise fixation on what was just seen. A 2026 meta-analysis finds the effect small, significant and not inevitable — it depends on the task and the interaction design. The interventions with measured effect are structural: named dimensions and tags, analogies, reusable exploration intents, real references instead of model defaults, low-fidelity outputs, and examples on request.

### Cited Findings
- [M] Doshi and Hauser (2024, Science Advances): writers given AI story ideas wrote stories rated more creative, better written and more enjoyable — most of all the less creative writers — but the AI-assisted stories were more similar to each other than stories written without AI. — [arXiv 2312.00506 (Dec 2023; journal Jul 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00506)
- [M] Anderson, Shah and Kreminski (Creativity & Cognition 2024), N=36: with ChatGPT, different users produced less semantically distinct ideas than with another creativity support tool; they produced more ideas with more detail, and felt less responsible for them. — [arXiv 2402.01536 (Feb 2024, rev. May 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01536)
- [M] Wadinambiarachchi et al. (CHI 2024), N=60: higher fixation with an AI image generator, no gain in number, variety or originality; fixation moved from the example to the AI's own outputs. — [arXiv 2403.11164 (Mar 2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11164v1)
- [M] Kumar et al. (CHI 2025), 1,100 participants: no originality benefit on divergent tasks, and worse unassisted performance afterwards; a secondary summary adds that exposed groups stayed more similar even after the AI was removed. — [arXiv 2410.03703 (Feb 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03703v2)
- [S][M] de Rooij and Biskjaer (2026, OSF preprint, not yet peer reviewed): meta-analysis of 18 articles, 19 experiments, 61 effect sizes (2022 to early 2026): a significant homogenization effect; strongest for idea generation on specific, defined problems; weaker for open-ended divergent thinking, writing and visual art; also visible, small but significant, in before-and-after comparisons of published essays and artworks; signs of carry-over to later tasks. The authors say it is not inevitable and depends on task, system and interaction design. Mostly text tools. — [PsyPost report (14 May 2026)](https://www.psypost.org/real-world-evidence-shows-generative-ai-is-making-human-creative-output-more-uniform/)
- [S][M] Wenger and Kenett (PNAS Nexus): 22 commercial chatbots against 102 people — each chatbot was about as creative as an average person, but the chatbots' answers were more like one another than the people's were; turning up randomness gave nonsense. — [PsyPost report (14 May 2026)](https://www.psypost.org/real-world-evidence-shows-generative-ai-is-making-human-creative-output-more-uniform/)
- [S] Design-specific signs, both preprints: AI-generated brand identities for unrelated products came back with similar cream backgrounds, earth-tone palettes, sans-serif type and short taglines (Choudhary, SSRN, Jan 2026); a study of generative UI tools examines whether they fall back on the same visual, color and layout defaults while presenting them as tailored choices ("Design Theater", arXiv Jul 2026). — [SSRN 7186758 (Jan 2026)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7186758); [arXiv 2607.22928 (Jul 2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.22928)
- [S] Chen et al. (arXiv Feb 2025) argue that generative models themselves show design fixation — outputs limited to familiar solutions — in both text and image models, and propose it as a lens for tools. — [arXiv 2502.05870 (Feb 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05870)
- [Q] Professionals' own reports: concept designers said image generators give similar-looking outputs across runs and a similar style every time (AIdeation); product designers said text-to-image tools invite small prompt edits rather than new directions (Inkspire). — [AIdeation](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1); [Inkspire](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18588v1)

#### Interventions with measured effect
- [M] Dimension and tag scaffolds: DesignWeaver's images were less alike (CLIP similarity 0.863 vs 0.903, p<0.001) and rated more novel by experts (4.09 vs 3.54, p=0.002). — [arXiv 2502.09867v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09867v2)
- [M] Reusable exploration intents: IdeaBlocks gave 2.13 times more images explored and 12.5% more visual diversity. — [arXiv 2507.22163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22163)
- [M] Analogies plus rough sketch underlays: Inkspire's prompts were less alike (0.51 vs 0.76) and exploration ratings higher. — [arXiv 2501.18588v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18588v1)
- [M] Keyword merging across references: CreativeConnect users merged more distant keywords (p=0.008) and leaned less on single reference pictures. — [arXiv 2312.11949v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11949v2)
- [M] Prompting the model itself: with GPT-4, plain prompting gave idea pools less diverse than groups of people; chain-of-thought prompting gave the most diverse pool and the most unique ideas of the prompts tested, close to human groups. — [Meincke, Mollick & Terwiesch, SSRN 4708466 (Jan 2024)](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4708466)
- [M] Pushing outputs away from consensus (text, preprint, N=16): semantic diversity up 85–167%, consensus phrases down 43–95%, with higher usefulness and coherence ratings. — [arXiv 2606.09587 (Jun 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09587)
- [M] Structure does not always reach the pictures: Brief2Design made prompts more varied but images no more varied than chat; Expandora raised prompt diversity (image diversity not reported in the abstract). — [arXiv 2604.11019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11019); [arXiv 2503.00791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00791)
- [M] Timing of examples: examples on request gave the most novel ideas; examples pushed at fixed intervals reduced output (Siangliulue et al. 2015); late examples increased conformity (Kulkarni et al. 2012). — [Siangliulue et al.](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/siangliulue15providing.pdf); [Kulkarni et al.](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/paper.php?id=206)
- [P] Recommended but untested in the source: low-fidelity or partial outputs, mixing unrelated ideas into prompts, reminders to consider alternatives (Wadinambiarachchi et al.). Low-fidelity output has support elsewhere: 12 of 16 CreativeConnect participants preferred sketches for this reason. — [arXiv 2403.11164](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11164v1); [arXiv 2312.11949v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11949v2)

### Inferences
- A private library of the designer's own picks is itself a defence: sameness comes from many people drawing on the same model defaults, and a mix grounded in one person's 300 chosen references starts from a different place. The risk moves to the generation step, where the model's defaults fill every gap the references leave unspecified (spacing, type scale, default radii, stock layouts). The more of the design that is pinned to named parts of real references, the less room the defaults have.
- "Fixation displacement" applies to a designer looking at AI mixes: after the first output, people anchor on it. Showing several structurally different directions at once, and keeping them rough, counters this.
- Variety in prompts does not guarantee variety in results (Brief2Design). Diversity should be checked on the outputs, not on the instructions.
- The meta-analysis result that defined problems homogenize most suggests the AI should be given the least freedom exactly where the brief is most specific, and the designer's references the most say.

### Gaps
- The meta-analysis was read through a news report; its pooled effect size was not given there and the preprint itself was not read.
- Doshi and Hauser's sample sizes and similarity figures were not in the abstract read.
- Almost all homogenization evidence is for text. For visual and interface design the evidence found is two 2026 preprints and designers' own reports; no peer-reviewed measurement of sameness in AI-assisted interface or web design was found.
- No study was found that tests whether grounding generation in a personal reference library reduces sameness.

## 7. Concrete workflow steps the evidence supports

### Takeaway
The evidence supports a step-by-step mixing workflow in which the AI never goes straight from several references to one finished design: split each reference into typed parts, state each chosen part as a principle, give the references roles with one in charge, check the chosen parts pairwise for fit, produce several rough directions with a note of what came from where, and have the designer judge each against written criteria and iterate. The first five steps have direct support from tested systems or experiments; the pairwise check and the novelty target are well-founded methods that no AI design study has yet tested.

### Cited Findings
Each step lists what supports it. Strength: **strong** = controlled measurement; **moderate** = qualitative study or abstract-level measurement; **method** = established method or theory, not tested in a design-AI study.

1. **Split every reference into typed, named parts tied to their source picture, and let the designer edit them.** (strong) Typed keywords raised the elements people found from 13.2 to 34.7; tags doubled the design vocabulary people used; professionals already think in typed parts (subject / pose / mood / arrangement; theme / contents / style / light / palette / angle). — [CreativeConnect](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11949v2); [DesignWeaver](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09867v2); [AIdeation](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1); [Brickify](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.21219)
2. **Before reusing a part, write it as a principle — what it does and why it works there — and where possible get the principle by comparing two references that share it.** (strong for ideas; untested for visual style) Schema-first idea generation beat raw examples; side-by-side comparison beat one-at-a-time study; a single example transfers poorly; an explicit abstract pattern took novices from 0.55 to 5.67 successful blends. — [Yu, Kittur & Kraut](https://bibtex.github.io/CHI-2014-YuKK14a.html); [Gentner et al.](https://typeset.io/pdf/learning-and-transfer-a-general-role-for-analogical-encoding-1j5d5qfzya.pdf); [Gick & Holyoak](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/items/196ea13e-fb69-4a74-a6f9-864c81aed465/full); [VisiBlends](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~chilton/web/my_publications/VisiBlends_CHI2019.pdf); [Hope et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1706.05585)
3. **Give the references roles: one hero that sets the frame, a few supporting references that each give a different kind of part, and small accents.** (moderate) Professionals sort references into Hero/Main, Detailed Supporting and Miscellaneous; blends organised by one input's frame are the simpler kind; unity is the dominant factor in appreciation; one dominant element with sub-dominant and subordinate levels is standard practice; one single uncommon example helped most in the meta-analysis. — [AIdeation](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1); [blending networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_blending); [Post et al.](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/to-preserve-unity-while-almost-allowing-for-chaos-testing-the-aes/); [Bradley](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2015/02/design-principles-dominance-focal-points-hierarchy/); [Sio et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.destud.2015.04.004)
4. **State the shared ground before mixing: what do these references have in common that lets them join? If nothing, change the set.** (strong for conceptual and visual blends) Finding the connecting concept first more than doubled blend ideas; the shape match was the condition for a successful visual blend, and pairs with no shared shape gave no blend until the search was redirected. — [PopBlends](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.04920); [VisiBlends](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~chilton/web/my_publications/VisiBlends_CHI2019.pdf)
5. **Lay the choices out as a grid (dimension × options taken from references) and check every pair of chosen options for fit — logical, then practical — before generating; say why a pair fails.** (method) Cross-consistency assessment removes incoherent combinations with a few hundred pair judgments for very large spaces; fixing one choice then shows what still fits. AI tools already build the grid but skip the check. — [Ritchey](https://www.swemorph.com/ma.html); [Luminate](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12953v3); [DesignWeaver](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09867v2)
6. **Produce several structurally different directions at once, rough and without full layout (a tile, a collage of fragments, a sketch with a one-line statement), not one polished page.** (strong) 12 of 16 designers preferred sketches; detailed AI images raised fixation; designers show about three alternatives; style tiles and element collages exist for this reason; finished outputs were named as a cause of fixation by professional designers. — [CreativeConnect](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11949v2); [Wadinambiarachchi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11164v1); [DesignWeaver](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09867v2); [styletil.es](https://styletil.es/); [Brief2Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11019)
7. **Show what came from where, grouped by meaning, with each transfer able to be switched off and its strength set.** (moderate) Misty's semantic diff groups changes and makes each a switch; AIdeation labels each result with its origin; Artinter's per-concept weights let people home in step by step, and the differences between steps told them what they wanted; a mismatch between what the person thinks "mix" does and what the model does was a named failure. — [Misty](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13900v3); [AIdeation](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1); [Artinter](https://johnr0.github.io/assets/publications/DIS2023-Artinter.pdf)
8. **Change one part at a time and keep the rest still; let the designer lock parts.** (moderate) AIdeation rewrites only the matching part of the description; its users' main complaint was not being able to hold an element while changing another; DesignPrompt's designers held the palette and varied the rest to get novelty with consistency; Misty's partial transfers (one button of several) show the need to apply a change to the whole set it belongs to. — [AIdeation](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1); [DesignPrompt summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03074v1); [Misty](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13900v3)
9. **Have the designer judge each direction against written criteria: Is it one thing rather than parts side by side? Can each source's contribution still be recognised? Does every borrowed part earn its place? Then iterate on the second-order features (make the borrowed color and style agree) before discarding a direction.** (strong for visual blends; theory for the rest) The two-criteria check and the three kinds of iteration are what VisiBlends' 96% rests on; fused and simple blends are liked most; the criteria match the Integration, Unpacking and Relevance principles. — [VisiBlends](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~chilton/web/my_publications/VisiBlends_CHI2019.pdf); [Schmid on governing principles](https://www.anglistik.uni-muenchen.de/personen/professoren/schmid/schmid_publ/blending_relevance.pdf)
10. **Aim for a mostly conventional base with one or two unusual parts, and give a "how far" control that is loose early and tight late.** (strong outside design; moderate inside) Conventional core plus unusual tail nearly doubled the hit rate in science; typicality and novelty both help preference when the other is held; nearer sources went with the best real-world design ideas while far ones raised novelty in the lab; tools use a creative score or diversity level for this, and users asked for chance early and determinism late. — [Uzzi et al.](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/uzzi/htm/papers/Science-2013-Uzzi-468-72.pdf); [Hekkert et al.](https://www.jimdavies.org/summaries/HekkertSneldersVanwieringen2003.html); [Chan, Dow & Schunn](https://joelchan.github.io/files/2014-desstudies-nearfar-in-press.pdf); [AIdeation](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1); [Misty](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13900v3)
11. **Bring references in early and when the designer asks, a few at a time, favouring uncommon ones; do not push them on a timer or late.** (strong) — [Siangliulue et al.](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/siangliulue15providing.pdf); [Kulkarni et al.](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/paper.php?id=206); [Sio et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.destud.2015.04.004)
12. **For an abstract goal ("protective", "calm"), turn it into concrete analogies first and pick among them.** (strong, one study) Exploration and inspiration rose and prompts became more varied. — [Inkspire](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18588v1)
13. **Guard variety on the outputs: ground every part in a real reference rather than a model default, ask the model to reason through distinct options before answering, and compare the directions for sameness before showing them.** (moderate) Homogenization is measurable but depends on interaction design; chain-of-thought prompting gave the most diverse idea pools; varied prompts did not always give varied images; AIdeation added real image search because designers distrusted and tired of model-made references. — [meta-analysis report](https://www.psypost.org/real-world-evidence-shows-generative-ai-is-making-human-creative-output-more-uniform/); [Meincke et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4708466); [Brief2Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11019); [AIdeation](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14747v1)
14. **Keep exploration moves that worked so they can be reused.** (moderate) Reusable intent blocks (property, direction, range) gave 2.13 times more exploration and 12.5% more visual diversity. — [IdeaBlocks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22163)

### Inferences
- Read as one flow: (a) designer or AI gathers a small set of references, early and on request; (b) each is split into typed parts; (c) chosen parts are written as principles; (d) one reference is named the frame, the others get distinct roles, and the shared ground is stated in one sentence; (e) the chosen parts are checked pairwise for fit, with reasons for any clash; (f) three or so rough, layout-free directions are produced that differ in structure, each with a note of what came from where; (g) the designer judges each against the written criteria, switches transfers on and off, locks what works; (h) iteration changes one part at a time; (i) only then is a full design made.
- Steps (c), (d), (e) and (g) are the ones current AI tools mostly lack, and they are the ones the cognition and blending literature points to as the difference between copying and combining. They are cheap in tool terms: they are text and judgment, not image generation.
- The human does three things the evidence says should stay human: picks the parts, names the frame, and judges the whole. The AI's tested strengths are surfacing parts people would miss, proposing combinations quickly, and producing rough renderings to react to.
- Expect gains in exploration and in the designer's sense of control first. No study yet shows that any of this raises expert-judged quality of the final design, so that should be checked on real work rather than assumed.

### Gaps
- No study has tested this whole flow end to end, nor steps 5 and 10 inside an AI design tool; they are inferences from methods and from evidence in other fields.
- No evidence was found on the right number of references in a mix, or the right number of directions to show (practice says about three; the studies showed 3–5).
- Nearly all system studies are single sessions of 30–60 minutes with 12–16 people and self-reported measures; AIdeation's one-week studio deployment is the only field evidence.
- Nothing found covers motion as a part to be mixed, or website references as opposed to still images.
